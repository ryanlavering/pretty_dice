What is real here

Exact: the surfaces are analytic: the original shape is Sloan's Gömböc r⁴ = 1 + 4β sinφ cos[θ - (3π/2)(cosφ - cos³φ/3)] (arXiv:2306.14914), and the "more pronounced" body is my own numerical construction (a degree-10 spherical-harmonic series for r⁴, optimised for flatness; see the design note). Both were checked to have exactly one stable and one unstable equilibrium and to be convex. The body rolls under the full rigid-body rolling-without-slipping equations (Newton-Euler on the true support function), RK4.

Physical vs convenience: the "rolling resistance" is a viscous torque, a stand-in for real rolling friction. The default (2.0) is 3.3 times the value of the first version so the roll ends in about 20 s of simulated time instead of 60; at every setting tested the body ends at the same point, only the time and the number of wobbles change. Time also runs faster than real (speed selector), and "Skip to rest" fast-forwards. No slipping, bouncing or air; the table is infinite; contact curvature uses finite differences (about 1e-9).

Approximate bodies (round 9): the bodies labelled r_max/r_min 1.12 to 1.25 are convex but have TWO stable points as uniform solids. They are made to end always on one point by shifting the centre of mass (the slider, default per body = the smallest shift found); that is a non-uniform density, not a true Gomboc. Exact: their surface (explicit formula, spherical-harmonic series of degree 12) and the rolling equations. Approximate: the mass distribution.

Shapes: Sloan's body deviates only about 3% from a sphere (r_max/r_min 1.06). The pronounced body has r_max/r_min = 1.10 (try "colour by radius": blue is the smallest radius, red the largest). Red dot: stable point (the "1"); blue dot: unstable point.
