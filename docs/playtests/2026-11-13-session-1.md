# Playtest 1: 2026-11-13, build 6b1bd27 (v4, branch playtest-1)

Cast: DM, Thorn Ironbrew (dwarf fighter, desktop), Mira Vane (elf wizard, desktop), Pip Thistledown (halfling rogue, phone-size 390x844).
Budget used: about 330k subagent tokens (the brief's 150k was too low; see Notes). All four agents were Sonnet.
Parent card: PD-142.14.1. Findings: PD-142.14.1.1 to .26.

Setup: each agent had its own Chromium profile and tab, driven through a small per-role command line driver. Agents got only a copy of docs/guide, README and CHANGELOG, and the app at a served worktree. The guide was checked first with `npm run guide`: 14 of 17 images identical, three differed by under 40 bytes (software GL noise), so the guide matches the UI.

## Rounds
0. Setup. Each agent read the guide, made a die look and a roll set (DM 3d20 and d20+5, Thorn d20+d8, Mira 3d6, Pip d20+d6+3), rolled once and found the roll log.
1. Party reaches a ruined watchtower at dusk: four goblins at a cookfire, a rigged door. Goblin initiative 4d20 = 19, 14, 4, 2 (+2 each: 21, 16, 6, 4); the shown total (39) was useless. Initiative: Mira 12, Thorn 9, Pip 7. Mira's cantrip found a blade trap (DC 13). Goblins swarmed Thorn (AC 17): two hits, 13 damage, Thorn 30 to 17.
2. Thorn hit (19+5) for 9, killing a goblin; his second swing missed. Mira's Firebolt hit (19) for 8 on 3d6, killing a second (her extra roll of 18 was disregarded). Pip disarmed the trap (11+3 = 14 vs 13). The two goblins left missed Thorn. The DM rolled a d8 door table (1) and a secret d20 for Pip, which showed openly and stayed in the log.
3. Thorn hit (17+5) for 7, dropping a goblin; the last fled. Mira failed Arcana (4+2 = 6 vs 12); Pip rolled a natural 1 on Perception (4 vs 12). Thorn opened the door; the DM's d8 table roll was 8: a chest, 40 gp and a silver boot. Final HP: Thorn 17/30, Mira 18/18, Pip 22/22.

## What the table wanted that the app did not have
Ranked by how many agents hit it:
1. Advantage and disadvantage (all four).
2. Changing dice at the table without Showcase or the bag (all four).
3. Roll labels and who rolled in the log (three).
4. Per-die and per-roll modifiers, and rolling several dice separately for group attacks and initiative (two).
5. Copy or export of the log (two).
6. Hidden DM rolls, table lookup, initiative order (one each, all DM).

What worked: custom dice looks, the Custom set editor (notation and +/- tiles), the total with per-die breakdown, the NATURAL 1 and NATURAL 20 labels, saved sets with the lightning quick roll, and the log once opened. A full six-round session fits the app: every roll the table needed was possible, with manual workarounds.

## Notes and caveats
- Excluded as environment or harness, not app findings: rolls taking 10-40 s and the page freezing for minutes after load and after a substrate change (software GL in this sandbox; re-check on a real GPU), the "UI paused" toast, screenshots capping at 500x644 so phone landscape was not tested, and the hex colour field timeouts (driver).
- The DM heard dice results only as reported, which worked but meant the DM could not verify any roll (itself a finding for hidden-information play).
- Players could re-roll a set freely (.16) and nothing recorded which roll was "the" roll, so the table runs on trust.
- Screenshot file names were reused across roles and overwrote each other, so the findings do not link screenshots. For the next playtest, give each role its own shots folder.
- Pip's phone scroll result used mouse drag, not touch. Verify on a real device.
- The brief's budget of about 150k tokens was too low for four agents on this renderer: setup alone cost about 60-70k per agent. Use roughly 400k for a six-round session on this setup.
